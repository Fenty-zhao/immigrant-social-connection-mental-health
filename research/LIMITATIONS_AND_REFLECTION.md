# Research Interpretation, Limitations and Next Questions

## Interpreting the central finding

The dissertation asked whether **social media could compensate for weaker offline friendships**, tested statistically as moderation of the friendship-quality/psychological-distress association.

The submitted results tell a specific story: friendship quality was associated with lower distress in the baseline individual fixed-effects model, while the social-media interaction was **not statistically significant**. A non-significant interaction is neither evidence that social media never provides emotional support nor proof that a compensatory effect is absent. It is a statement about the **evidence available in these measures and models**.

This distinction matters because the dissertation did not directly observe the quality, recipient or emotional meaning of online connections.

## Measurement and research design limitations

**The social-media measures capture frequency, not relationships.** UKHLS records how often respondents look at or post on social media. That does not distinguish maintaining close relationships across borders from passive browsing or socially comparative use. Consequently, the survey cannot directly test which kinds of online contact provide psychological support.

**Two survey waves limit the design.** Wave 11 and Wave 14 support a within-person comparison across two time points, but they cannot reveal detailed trajectories of adaptation, platform use or mental health between observations. The matched panel and complete-case regression sample also differ, raising potential concerns about attrition and missingness.

**Fixed effects address a specific confounding problem.** Individual fixed effects control for stable, time-invariant differences between respondents. They do **not** control for every change in housing, health, life circumstances or other time-varying influences that may coincide with both social connectedness and mental health.

**The COVID comparison is a period contrast.** Wave 11 and Wave 14 span the pandemic period, but a survey-wave interaction is not a randomised or independently identified causal effect of COVID-19.

**Subgroups may differ.** Immigrants vary by migration purpose, stage of settlement, family circumstances, length of residence and social contexts. The submitted study did not establish separate, reliable effects for all these groups.

## From evidence to better research questions

A natural follow-up is not simply to obtain **more data about frequency**; it is to measure the **function and quality of digital relationships** more directly.

For example, future data collection could distinguish whether someone uses online platforms to:

- maintain close family and friendship ties across borders;
- develop supportive relationships in the host country;
- seek help or share personal difficulties;
- browse content without reciprocal communication.

These are **proposed future measurements**, not variables analysed in the submitted dissertation.

A mixed-methods follow-up could explore *why* different forms of online connection feel supportive or insufficient, then use better-targeted survey items or panel data to evaluate the patterns quantitatively. This is a forward-looking proposal rather than work claimed as completed.

## Research skills demonstrated

The transferable practices evidenced by the dissertation include: defining a **moderation hypothesis**; deciding between cross-sectional and within-person modelling; assembling panel observations; specifying interactions and controls; comparing alternative model specifications; and reporting a null hypothesis test without exaggeration.

The most important substantive distinction is between **access to digital platforms**, **frequency of use**, and **meaningful interpersonal connection**. A measure of one should not automatically stand in for another.
